Write the clinical note for the consultation transcript below in formal clinical prose - no section headers, no bullet points - as TWO to THREE paragraphs: first the history (presenting complaint, the symptoms asked about with the answers given, relevant background, and any medications or allergies that were discussed) with the examination findings that were explicitly stated; then a final paragraph with the stated assessment, if any, and the plan, including any follow-up and what the patient was told to come back for.

FIRST, decide whether this transcript is a healthcare consultation with a patient. If it is clearly not - for example a lecture, a video, a phone call or conversation about something else, or one person talking with no patient present - write exactly one line and nothing else:
NOT A CONSULTATION: <the reason, under ten words>
Only refuse when it is obvious; a short, noisy or incomplete consultation still gets a note. Otherwise, write the note as follows.

The note is the consulting professional's own record, so whoever held the consultation is never mentioned. The words "doctor", "clinician" and "GP" must not appear anywhere in the note. Write only what was decided or done for the patient in the passive voice ("was advised", "was prescribed", "was arranged", "was referred", "was suspected"), never naming who did it. Vary how sentences begin; do not start every sentence with "The patient". Avoid sentences over 30 words.
Record examination FINDINGS only - what was seen or felt - never the steps taken to examine. If no examination was described in the transcript, write nothing about examination.

Never write the patient's name, date of birth or address - the note identifies its patient by where it is filed, not by the text. Refer to them only as "the patient". Open with "The patient presented with...". Give the age or sex only if the transcript says it; never guess either, and use "he" or "she" only when the transcript makes the sex clear.

CRITICAL RULES - follow exactly:
- Use ONLY facts explicitly stated in the transcript. If something was not said, do not write it.
- Do NOT add any normal or negative finding unless it was explicitly spoken. Never write "no fever", "denies chest pain", "no blood in stool", "no swelling", etc. unless that exact negative was actually stated.
- Reproduce every stated negative exactly as said. Never flip a positive to a negative or a negative to a positive.
- Write a symptom as absent ONLY when that symptom was asked about and the patient clearly said no. If the patient described the symptom at any point, it is present. If an answer changed, give the patient's final answer. If an answer is unclear or garbled in the transcript, leave that symptom out.
- Mention medication or allergies ONLY if they were asked about or the patient raised them. If allergies were not discussed, say nothing about allergies.
- Never state a diagnosis that was not given in the consultation. If none was given, say that no diagnosis was made.
- Do NOT invent or infer: dates, ages, vital signs, measurements, drug doses, diagnoses, test results, or family history.
- The assessment and plan may contain ONLY what was said in the consultation - never add your own advice, differential diagnoses, or management suggestions.
- Attribute reported symptoms to the patient, varying the wording ("reported", "described", "noticed").

Style: formal, objective, third person, chronological (start at onset). Write in the past tense - the consultation has ended ("presented with", "reported", "examination revealed", "was advised"); standing facts such as long-term conditions stay present. Use British English spelling. Correct minor grammar but keep every medical fact exact. Ignore non-medical conversation.

Group all the stated details of one complaint into a single sentence - its onset, timing, character, location, and severity together - rather than a separate sentence for each detail. Do not add any detail that was not stated.
